Is it Poisson? No — and the size of the failure is the point

Loading…
A Poisson process has variance equal to its mean, and that is forced, not conventional: if the rate is fixed and posts don't influence each other, arrival timing is the only randomness left, so one number sets both the typical level and the scatter. When the data refuses that constraint, at least one assumption is false — either the rate is not fixed (some days he is simply a different person) or the posts are not independent (one post pulls the next along). Both suspects are modelled, and both are on the leaderboard.

The tail

When the largest observation is many times the median, the average stops being a stable summary: collect more data, feel confident, then one enormous day rewrites the estimate. That is why every number on this site is a median with an asymmetric interval.

Does the process remember yesterday?

Why the cycle is removed first. The raw series has a strong weekly rhythm, so its raw autocorrelation shows a lag-7 spike no matter what. That spike is the calendar reflected back at you, not evidence the process remembers a week ago. Removing the known cycle before looking for memory is the difference between a finding and a mirror.

Choosing which slice of history to model is a bigger lever on the answer than choosing the model, and it is usually decided by accident. Fitting across a genuine regime change describes neither regime — the way an average of a man's height across his lifetime describes him at no age at all. The window used here is stated on every page.

Bursts

Descriptive, not causal. The keywords below say what he was posting about on a heavy day. They do not explain the heavy day. A self-exciting process produces bursts with no outside trigger whatever, and whatever he happened to be posting about will always be sitting there ready to take the blame. Telling a genuine external cause apart from a self-feeding run is not settled from daily totals, and this page does not pretend otherwise.
