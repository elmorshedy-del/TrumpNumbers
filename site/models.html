How the models actually score

Every model is fitted only on the past, then asked to forecast a week it has never seen — for each of the seven days you might ask on. Scores below come from … such forecasts.

Two different virtues, and you need both. Calibration asks whether an 80% interval really contains the truth 80% of the time — is the model honest? Sharpness asks how narrow the interval is — is it useful? A forecast of "somewhere between 0 and 500" is perfectly calibrated and perfectly useless. The goal is the sharpest model that is still honest, which is why a model can win on error and still be disqualified by the calibration column.

Leaderboard

Skill is the fraction of climatology's error removed; 0 means "no better than resampling history". Sharpness against honesty

The number on the front page, scored

The tracker does not show a model. It shows a rule — average the top three of this leaderboard, quantile-wise — and until now that composite appeared on no row of the board it was built from. Its members' scores are not its score: a combination can be worse than its best member. So the rule is replayed week by week, choosing its models using only weeks that had already closed, and graded like everything else.

Does the forecast improve as the week unfolds?

Mean CRPS by how much of the week was already banked when the forecast was made — from nothing at all, which is what the tracker shows for a seventh of every week, to six days done. Most of the drop across this row is the calendar handing over the answer, not a model getting cleverer, so the second table divides out climatology at the same cut. That is the comparison that isolates the model.
